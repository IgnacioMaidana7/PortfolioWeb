# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers scanning dozens of developer profiles. They spend 30-60 seconds deciding whether to reach out. Success means they bookmark the page, forward the CV, or write directly. They are non-technical or semi-technical; they respond to clarity, confidence, and memorable first impressions over deep technical detail.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Ignacio Maidana, Frontend Developer and Systems Engineering student based in Córdoba, Argentina. The portfolio communicates technical capability, leadership in agile project management, and professional maturity. It exists to convert a first visit into a contact or interview opportunity.

## Brand Personality

Bold · Creative · Precise. Direct without being cold. Confident without being loud. The visual quality of the portfolio is itself a demonstration of frontend skill — recruiters should feel the competence before they read a word.

## Anti-references

- Generic dark-mode developer portfolio: midnight navy + neon green or blue, matrix code rain, monospace-everything aesthetic. Too common, zero differentiation.
- Corporate LinkedIn clone: resume converted to HTML with bullet points and no visual personality. Feels like a PDF export, not a crafted product.
- Over-animated playground: the site becomes a showcase of Framer Motion rather than of the developer's work. Motion should be purposeful.
- Design-portfolio pretender: ornamental, illustration-heavy, looks like a UI designer's work rather than an engineer's — sends the wrong signal to technical recruiters.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** The visual quality of the portfolio must demonstrate frontend competence directly. A recruiter who can't evaluate React code can evaluate whether the site feels professional and deliberate.
2. **Precision over decoration.** Every element earns its place. Spacing, weight contrast, and alignment do more work than decorative elements.
3. **Bold confidence, not noise.** Stand out through clear hierarchy and committed visual choices — not through animations that shout or colors that startle.
4. **Work is the hero.** Projects and skills are the reason the portfolio exists. The interface serves the content, never competes with it.
5. **One earned surprise.** A single memorable detail — a motion moment, a typographic choice, a layout beat — that makes the site stick in memory. Not scattered throughout.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Full keyboard navigation. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Color contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for body text, ≥ 3:1 for large text. Icon-only controls must have accessible labels.
